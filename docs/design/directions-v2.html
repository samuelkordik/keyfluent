Keyfluent: round 2

Four takes on C · Boot screen. All four use all-caps Krypton for display text, Argon for body and the terminal, and Radon for teaching notes. They all keep A's function-key bar, its box-titled panels (terminal, goal) and its progress dots. The theme colors the whole app. Versions 1, 2 and 4 use Tomorrow Night Bright; version 3 uses Tomorrow Night Eighties, to show that a theme recolors everything.

Fun features shared by all four (feel like play, never homework):

  • A score line after every card: the grade word, your time against the target, and the shortest route if yours was longer.
  • New best when you beat your own record on a card.
  • A combo count for correct answers in a row. It ends quietly, with no "you broke it" message.
  • An end-of-session summary.
  • Practice days shown as "days this month", never as a streak you can lose.
  • Scenarios unlock as bonus rounds once their cards are solid.

1 · Arcade cabinet

C, tightened. An arcade HUD across the top: card, combo, your best, the target. The result flash is a score line; the session ends on a results table.

Layout
Stripe, HUD, intent, terminal + goal panels, dots, F-key bar. Today is a title screen; Progress and Preferences are full screens (F3, F4)
Color
Each category owns one accent (Basics blue, Copy mode green, Sessions purple, Layout orange). HUD labels in red, like arcade score labels
Motion
The score counts up on the result flash; the stripe pulses on a new best. Both are off with reduced motion
Signature
The HUD and the end-of-session results table
Risk
The HUD adds a row of numbers to read during practice. The HUD can be hidden in Preferences

2 · Home computer

The window is an 8-bit micro's screen: a thick colored border around the work area, uppercase system text, and "READY." on the Today screen.

Layout
A 22 px border around everything. Inside: the same practice layout as version 1, without the HUD
Color
The border takes the category's color. On a correct answer it flashes loading-stripes (a static flash with reduced motion); on a miss it turns red with the word "Again"
Copy voice
System lines in caps ("12 REVIEWS DUE"); cards and teaching notes in normal case, for readability
Signature
The border: you see whether you got it right from the edge of your eye, without reading
Risk
The flashing stripes are intense. They are limited to under 3 flashes a second (WCAG 2.3.1) and can be turned off. The border also costs 44 px of width

3 · Cartridge

16-bit console era. Stars per card, and the Progress page as a stage-select grid: every card is a tile, colored by how well you know it.

Stars = ratings
The FSRS grade, shown as stars: Again ☆☆☆, Hard ★☆☆, Good ★★☆, Easy ★★★. Each card keeps your best stars
Progress
The spec's mastery heatmap becomes the stage select: rows per category, a tile per card (new, learning, young, mature). Select a tile to see its times and practice that card. Scenarios show as locked bonus tiles until their cards are solid
Color
Mastery bands carry the color; tiles also carry a letter or ★, so color is never the only signal
Signature
Stars and the stage-select grid: progress you can see filling in
Risk
Stars are the most overtly game-like element in this round. It works only if they map honestly to the grades (they do: 0–3 stars = the four FSRS ratings)

4 · Cracktro

The Today screen is a demoscene intro: raster bars, a sine-wave logo, a starfield, and a scrolling message that carries tips and the credits. Practice itself stays calm.

Today
Raster bars in the theme's six accents. The logo waves. The menu sits in the middle. The bottom scroller loops tips ("The prefix is Ctrl-b…") and greetings to the theme and font authors (the About credits, scene-style)
Practice
Same layout as version 1. On success, a raster bar sweeps once across the window behind the grade word
Session end
An "end part": the summary scrolls past over the starfield; any key returns to Today
Optional extra
Short chiptune sounds for correct, new best and session end (off by default, a Preferences switch)
Signature
The app opens with a show. Nobody forgets it
Risk
Heavy motion: with reduced motion everything is still (bars static, logo flat, scroller becomes a line of text). The Today screen redraws every frame, so it costs CPU while open; it can pause when the window is in the background
